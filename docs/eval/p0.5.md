# Evaluation after P0.5: structure-aware chunking

P0.5 changed how documents are chunked and nothing else. A long GDPR article is now one chunk per numbered paragraph, and an internal policy one chunk per section. Each piece is prefixed with its heading and title (`Art. 12 GDPR | Transparent information, … | 3. …`, `Data Retention Policy | Version 1.0 | Section 2. …`). This file compares the run on the new chunks with the [P0.1 baseline](baseline.md): the same 48 frozen questions, the same denominators, group by group.

## What changed

- **The chunk with the answer now reaches the model far more often.** In a retrieval-only check (below), the chunk holding an expected fact passes the filter for 34 of the 40 expected facts, against 24. Art. 12 passes for 10 of the 17 questions that list it, against none: its paragraph 3 ("within one month") and paragraph 5 ("free of charge") are much closer to the questions about them than the whole article was (v1-22: 0.993 against 1.342; no_conflict_expected-01: 1.088 against 1.578).
- **Fewer questions stop at the filter.** 7 questions retrieve nothing under 1.2, against 17; 3 of them are answerable, against 10.
- **No out-of-corpus question was answered**, as before (14 of 14 refused). But the filter now refuses 4 of them and the model 10, against 7 and 7: paragraph chunks sit closer to more questions, so more of them reach the model, and refusing them depends more on the model's own judgement (→ P0.4's refusal sentinel).
- **A policy section still moves tiers.** Retention §3 ("access requests … within 60 days") passes for five GDPR-only questions about data-subject requests (erasure, fees, an ignored request) and turns GOLD into SILVER. The whole policy did that to six questions: three of them still go SILVER through §3, one is fixed (no_conflict_expected-03), and two now miss earlier, at the model's refusal (v1-09, paraphrase-07). The other two of the five are new (v1-05, paraphrase-04).

## The crowding check

With 26 chunks instead of 6, the top 5 is a real limit: several paragraphs of one article could fill it and push out a chunk the answer needs. Before the change, we agreed to measure this and to propose any fix as a separate change.

**Method.** Retrieval only, no model calls: for every question, all 26 chunks ranked by distance, with the old chunker and the new one. A source is *crowded out* when it is under the 1.2 cutoff but ranked below 5th, so `/query` never sees it.

| | Baseline chunks | P0.5 chunks |
|---|---|---|
| Expected (document, heading) pairs that pass the filter | 26 of 54 | 41 of 54 |
| … crowded out | 0 | 0 |
| Expected facts whose answer-holding chunk passes the filter | 24 of 40 | 34 of 40 |
| … crowded out | 0 | 0 |

**Nothing was crowded out.** The breach questions do fill their top 5 mostly with Art. 33 paragraphs (conflict-01: 33(1), 33(3), retention §2, 33(5), 33(4)), but the chunks they lost are over the cutoff on their own: the draft's breach section at 1.256 there, 10th. A larger top 5 would not change those tiers, so the data gives no reason for a per-document cap.

The check agrees with the eval: its per-question count of "every expected source passed" is 12/34 on the old chunks (the baseline's sources figure, question by question) and 22/34 on the new ones (this run's). One limit: an answer-holding chunk is found by phrase, like the facts metric, so "legal obligation" also matches Art. 17(1)(e), a ground *for* erasure, on paraphrase-07.

## What the sources metric now measures

A chunk matches an expected source when it comes from that document and contains its heading. After the split, every paragraph of an article contains the article's heading, so any paragraph counts: v1-09 scores "sources" correct although the paragraph with the answer, Art. 17(3), did not pass. For policies the metric became stricter: only the right section counts, where the whole policy used to. Read the sources column with that in mind; facts, refusal and tier do not depend on headings.

## Limitations of this run

- One run of a non-deterministic model with no cached answers to reuse: a question or two may move on a re-run for reasons unrelated to chunking.
- 48 questions, one author; one question moves an overall figure by about 2 points.
- The five regressions are real, and P0.5 deliberately fixes none of them: changing the chunker now, after seeing these results, would be tuning it to them. v1-09, 0.003 over the cutoff, belongs to P3.2's cutoff sweep; the possible follow-ups below are separate changes, each to be measured on its own.

## Possible follow-ups (not done)

- **Paragraphs that depend on their article**, such as Art. 17(3). Sending a passing paragraph's whole article to the model would reach the exceptions, but the model would then read text that never passed the filter, which breaks the one-filter rule; it needs its own decision. P3.2's cutoff sweep covers v1-09 (0.003 over), not paraphrase-07.
- **Retention §3 as a near neighbour of every data-subject-request question**: a semantic match, not a chunking error; P3.2's sweep shows what a lower cutoff would trade for it.
- **The draft's breach section** is now over the cutoff for every breach question. P2.3's compliance card for the breach question needs it; worth measuring there before the demo relies on it.
